02Overview

Scent, on cue

Monklap is a wall-mounted or desktop diffuser shaped like a friendly monkey. Walk up and clap, and it responds with a pulse of fragrant mist from its mouth.

No buttons, no app, nothing to touch.

01

Touchless

Sound and proximity replace the spray trigger, for moments when your hands are busy, wet or full.

02

Interactive

An OLED face gives it a character and shows visible feedback for every state.

03

Ambient

A wick-fed ultrasonic atomizer releases a 1.5-second burst of mist on demand, not a constant stream.

03Target users

Monklap is for…

Three groups who want scent to feel like a moment of play, not another chore.

Aesthetic & tech enthusiasts

Gen Z and millennials looking for desk-friendly home objects with sound-driven, physical interaction rather than another standard IoT device.

07Sketch & ideation

From a button to a clap

Monklap began as a “Sound & Scent System”: a comfort tool for public spaces that played music as people walked by, then sprayed fragrance on their wrists at the press of a button.

KEPTWalk-up wake, a digital face, scent as a reward.
CHANGEDThe button became a clap; the form became a monkey.

08Process & lessons

Process & lessons

MODELING

Plan specifically

Taking Monklap from rough sketches to 3D models in Rhino taught me that the more specific a plan is, the less time I lose troubleshooting changes later. I now understand not only how to model objects for real-world use, but also how to plan a project so each stage of making flows into the next with less friction.

PRINTING

Bambu Lab A1

I printed Monklap's parts from my Rhino models on a Bambu Lab A1 using Bambu Studio. Printing itself went smoothly, but some edges warped, so I had to sand those surfaces flat.

SOLDERING

Reopen, resolder, glue

I troubleshot shorts caused by broken cords, cold solder joints and tangled stripped wires. Each time, I reopened the board, resoldered the joints and added hot glue so the same problem wouldn't happen again.

ASSEMBLY

Four rounds

Using the joint structures I designed during modeling, I fitted the sensors, display and atomizer in order. My plans for the circuits and wick didn't always match reality, so it took a lot of trial and error. After four rounds of printing and fitting, I arrived at the current Monklap.

09Final prototype

Monklap, awake.

The finished Monklap: live OLED eyes, ultrasonic sensors in the nostrils and an atomizer in the mouth, all running on an ESP32.
